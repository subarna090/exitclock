About Exit Clock

Simple answers for a difficult week.

The real answers exist — buried in government portals and written in language nobody wants to decode during the worst week of their working life.

Who wrote this

Subarna Das — tech professional with more than a decade in the IT industry, and a manager who had to tell people what came next.

Why I built this

Someone close to me signed a settlement without asking for a component-wise breakdown. By the time we realised what was missing, the people who could have fixed it had stopped replying.

Then I started seeing the same gaps everywhere — missed deadlines, benefits ending without warning, and people accepting full-and-final figures they couldn't actually verify.

I got tired of explaining the same things over WhatsApp. So I looked for something India-specific to share. What I found was either American advice that didn't apply here or ₹99 PDFs stitched together from blogs.

The real answers exist — buried in government portals and written in language nobody wants to decode during the worst week of their working life.

So I built this to make those answers simple, practical, and usable when people actually need them.

What I am, and what I am not

I am not a lawyer, a chartered accountant, an insurance adviser or a financial planner. Nothing here is advice about your specific situation, and I would be cautious of anyone selling that online for a fixed price.

What this kit does is narrower and, I think, more useful: it tells you which questions to ask, which deadlines start when, and where the official rule actually lives — so that when you do talk to HR, or a CA, or a lawyer, you already know what you are asking about.

How the research was done

Every regulatory statement in Module 3 comes from a primary official source, not from a blog summary or another kit. The sources are listed in Appendix D of the playbook and include:

  • Ministry of Labour & Employment — the four Labour Codes and the associated FAQs
  • EPFO — member FAQs, claim forms and portal guidance
  • Income Tax Department — provident fund TDS, retirement benefits, leave encashment
  • IRDAI — health insurance framework, migration and portability
  • ESIC — unemployment benefit schemes
  • National Career Service, and the Ministry of Health for Tele-MANAS

Each claim carries the date it was verified. All regulatory claims were last re-verified on 16 September 2026. Where a rule is in flux, the kit says so and tells you to check the live portal rather than trusting a printed number.

Why it will not be right forever

Indian labour, tax and insurance rules change, and the four Labour Codes — in force since 21 November 2025 — changed several of them at once. A guide like this decays. My response is a published changelog and a review every quarter: when something changes, the kit is corrected, the changelog records what changed and when, and everyone who bought the current edition gets an email.

The changelog is public, whether or not you have bought anything: CHANGELOG_URL_PLACEHOLDER

One worked example of how that works in practice: the September 2026 edition originally said ESIC's Atal Beemit Vyakti Kalyan Yojana had last been extended through 30 June 2026, which read as though the scheme might have lapsed. It had not — it was extended for a further year, to 30 June 2027. That was corrected, and the correction is in the changelog.

What I will never do

  • Promise you a job, an interview, a settlement figure or eligibility for a government benefit.
  • Tell you a rule without telling you where it comes from and when it was checked.
  • Publish anyone's layoff story, screenshot or company name to sell copies.
  • Ask you to paste your salary, your exit letter or your family's details into anything I control. The Exit Clock runs entirely in your browser for exactly this reason.
